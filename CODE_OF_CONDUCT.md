# Code of Conduct

This Code of Conduct applies to everyone who takes part in the Obsrv community — contributors, maintainers, adopters and anyone joining our discussions. Contributing to the project means you agree to uphold these principles.

## Our principles

* **Be considerate.** Your work and words affect users and other contributors. Think about how they will land, and remember that many people in this community work in a second or third language.
* **Be respectful.** Disagree about ideas, not people. Harassment, personal attacks, and discriminatory or exclusionary language are not acceptable.
* **Be available.** If you take on an issue or a review, keep others informed of your progress. If you need to step back, say so, so someone else can pick the work up.
* **Be honest.** Be open about what you know and what you don't, credit other people's work, and be upfront about the limitations of your contributions.
* **Exercise discretion and confidentiality at appropriate times.** Don't share other people's private information. Report security vulnerabilities privately rather than in public issues, and respect anything shared with you in confidence.

## Where this applies

These principles apply in every project space: the [Obsrv GitHub repositories](https://github.com/orgs/Sunbird-Obsrv/repositories), [GitHub Discussions](https://github.com/orgs/Sunbird-Obsrv/discussions), the [Discord channel](https://discord.gg/Q5mvw2mGC8), the [Jira issue tracker](https://project-sunbird.atlassian.net/jira/software/c/projects/OB/issues), community calls and events.

They also apply when you are representing the Obsrv community in public — speaking at an event, posting from a project account, or acting as a named maintainer elsewhere.

## Reporting a concern

If you experience or witness behaviour that breaks this Code of Conduct, report it privately to **info@sunbird.org**.

Please do not raise harassment reports in a public issue or discussion thread. The forum is a public space, and a public report exposes the person reporting as much as the person reported.

When you report, include whatever you can: what happened, where it happened, when, and links if the behaviour is on a public channel. You do not need to have evidence of a pattern — a single incident is enough to report.

## What happens next

* You will get an acknowledgement within **three working days**.
* A member of the [Obsrv Working Group](https://github.com/orgs/Sunbird-Obsrv/discussions/27) who is not involved in the report will review it. If a report concerns a Working Group member, that person takes no part in handling it.
* We will talk to you before taking any action, so you are not surprised by the outcome.
* Depending on what we find, the response may be a private warning, a request for a public apology, temporary removal from a project space, or a permanent ban.

Reports are held in confidence. We will not share your identity with the person reported without asking you first.
